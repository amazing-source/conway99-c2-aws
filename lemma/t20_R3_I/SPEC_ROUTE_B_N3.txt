==============================================================================================================

Laptop session (Claude), 2026-10-07. This page is self-contained: a program written from it alone is a second,
independent program for the branch n_3 = 2. Every statement of the model is proved here in one or two lines, or is a
fact of the project model quoted in section 1. Nothing here is a result; the claim to be tested is in section 9.

Difference with lemma/t20_HAND/SPEC_N3.md (route A): there the base is S u {p} u X_3 u X_0' (29 vertices) and all
twelve orbits of X_2 are enumerated. Here the base is S u N[p] u X_3 (37 vertices): the fourteen neighbours of p are in
the base, the set X_0' is NOT in the vertex set, and only the orbits of X_2 outside N(p) (8 or 9 of them) are

1. Setting
----------

G is a strongly regular graph with parameters (99, 14, 1, 2), adjacency matrix A: A^2 = 12 I - A + 2 J.
sigma is an automorphism of order 2 with exactly one fixed vertex p.

Facts (M), from the project model:
- For v != p: v ~ sigma v if and only if v is in N(p). N(p) is seven disjoint "mate edges" {w, sigma w}; inside N(p)
  a vertex is adjacent to its mate only (N(p) induces a perfect matching).
- A vertex outside N[p] is a "word": it is not adjacent to its mate and it has exactly two neighbours in N(p), in two
  different mate edges.
- lambda = 1: two adjacent vertices have exactly one common neighbour. mu = 2: two non-adjacent vertices have exactly two.
- Consequence: a vertex other than p is never adjacent to both ends of a mate edge (they already share p).

Case I of T20: S+ = {x_0, ..., x_9}, S- = {y_i = sigma x_i}, both independent, S = S+ u S- disjoint from N[p], and
x_i ~ y_j if and only if i ~ j in a 4-regular graph B on {0, ..., 9}. B is one of six graphs I.0 ... I.5 (section 8).

For q outside S, d(q) = |N(q) n S+| = |N(q) n S-|. X_d = {q outside S : d(q) = d}; a_j(q) = |N(q) n X_j|. p is in X_0.

    a_1 + 2 a_2 + 3 a_3 = 20 - 5d,        a_0 = a_2 + 2 a_3 + 3d - 6.                                   (G0)

(Proof: evaluate A^2 1_{S+} at q.) X_d is empty for d >= 4. Branch treated here: |X_3| = 2; then |X_0| = 7,
|X_1| = 46, |X_2| = 24.

Proved earlier, used here:
(P1) No vertex of N(p) is in X_3 (round 2, I.1). So X_3 = {z, z' = sigma z} with z a word: z !~ p, z !~ z'.
(P2) Every x_i has exactly two neighbours in N(p) (it is a word), never two mates.
(P3) Profile: h_j = number of mate edges of N(p) inside X_j. h_3 = 0 by (P1), and by (P2) h_1 + 2 h_2 = 10, so
     (h_2, h_1, h_0) is (3, 4, 0), (4, 2, 1) or (5, 0, 2); (5, 0, 2) is impossible (round 2, I.3, proved for n_3 = 2 too).
(P4) a_3(z) = 0 and a_2(z) <= 1 (lab notes H1 (a): a_0(z) = a_2(z) + 3 by (G0); the neighbours of z in X_0 avoid p, those
     of z and of z' have at most 2 common elements, and X_0 minus p has 6 vertices: 2 a_0(z) - 2 <= 6).
(P5) No vertex of X_2 is adjacent to both z and z' (N(z) n N(z') is one sigma-orbit; inside X_2 it would give a_2(z) >= 2).


2. The base (37 vertices)
-------------------------

Y = S u {p} u N(p) u {z, z'}.

Data of a base:
(a) the type (U3 ; V3) of z: z ~ x_i for i in U3, z ~ y_j for j in V3, |U3| = |V3| = 3. Then z' ~ y_i (i in U3) and
    z' ~ x_j (j in V3). The possible types, up to Aut(B) and the exchange z <-> z', are listed in section 8 (25 classes;
    they are exactly the types for which the 23 vertices S u {p, z, z'} pass the budget test).
(b) the "N-configuration": the seven mate edges of N(p) with their neighbours in S. A mate edge {w, sigma w} is
      of kind A, type {U, V}: w ~ x_U and y_V, sigma w ~ x_V and y_U, with U, V two disjoint 2-subsets of indices;
      of kind W, type {i, k}, i != k: w ~ x_i and y_k, sigma w ~ x_k and y_i;
      of kind Z: no neighbour in S.
    There are h_2 edges of kind A, h_1 of kind W, h_0 of kind Z, (h_2, h_1, h_0) = (3, 4, 0) or (4, 2, 1), and every
    index 0..9 lies in exactly two of the sets U u V (kind A) and {i, k} (kind W).
    Proof: a vertex w of N(p) has d(w) in {0, 1, 2} by (P1). If w ~ x_i and w ~ y_i then sigma w ~ y_i and x_i, and x_i
    would be adjacent to both ends of a mate edge: so U n V is empty and i != k. The covering statement is (P2).
(c) the attachment: the two neighbours w1, w2 of z in N(p); they lie in two different mate edges. Then z' ~ sigma w1,
    sigma w2. (z is a word.)

Why the induced graph on Y is determined by (a), (b), (c): every pair of classes.

    S+ with S+, S- with S-      none                                   case I
    S+ with S-                  x_i ~ y_j iff i ~_B j                  definition of B
    p with S                    none                                   S is disjoint from N[p]
    p with N(p)                 all 14                                 definition
    N(p) with N(p)              the seven mate edges only              (M)
    N(p) with S                 the types                              data (b)
    z, z' with S                the type (U3 ; V3)                     data (a)
    z, z' with p; z with z'     none                                   (P1): z is a word
    z with N(p)                 exactly w1, w2; z' : sigma w1, sigma w2    data (c); a word has exactly 2 neighbours in N(p)

Stage 1 = the list of all bases. For each type (a): ALL N-configurations (b) and ALL 84 attachments (c) (2 vertices among
14, not in the same mate edge) must be considered, up to the symmetries of section 6, and each base is either rejected
by an exact test of section 5 or kept. One extra rejection is allowed, by (P4): an attachment in which w1 and w2 are both
vertices of mate edges of kind A (then a_2(z) >= 2). In the first program this filter removes nothing: such attachments
already fail the budget test.


3. The orbits of X_2 outside N(p)
---------------------------------

X_2 has 24 vertices. The 2 h_2 vertices of the mate edges of kind A are in X_2 and are in the base. The others form
X_2' : 24 - 2 h_2 words, i.e. 9 sigma-orbits for profile (3, 4, 0) and 8 for profile (4, 2, 1).
For s in X_2: U_s = {i : s ~ x_i}, V_s = {j : s ~ y_j}, |U_s| = |V_s| = 2, U_{sigma s} = V_s.

Units. x_i and x_j (i != j) are not adjacent, so they have exactly two common neighbours. Those in S are the y_l with l a
common B-neighbour of i and j. z is one if i, j are both in U3; z' is one if both are in V3. All the others are in X_2
(a common neighbour outside S has d >= 2, and X_3 = {z, z'}). So the number of s in X_2 with U_s = {i, j} is exactly

    need(i, j) = 2 - codeg_B(i, j) - [i, j in U3] - [i, j in V3],

and the sum of need over all pairs is 24. A mate edge of kind A and type {U, V} uses one unit U (the vertex w) and one
unit V (the vertex sigma w). What is left, need'(i, j) = need(i, j) - (number of vertices of N(p) adjacent to x_i and
x_j), sums to 24 - 2 h_2 and is realised exactly by the vertices of X_2'.

Data of an orbit {s, sigma s} of X_2' (everything else follows by sigma):
- U_s and V_s, with need'(U_s) > 0, need'(V_s) > 0 and V_s != U_s (if V_s = U_s = {i, j}, the four vertices x_i, x_j,
  y_i, y_j are common neighbours of s and sigma s);
- N(s) n N(p): exactly two vertices, in two different mate edges (s is a word);
- [s ~ z], [s ~ z']: not both, by (P5);
- s !~ p, s !~ sigma s (word);
- for every other orbit {t, sigma t} of X_2' : the two bits [s ~ t], [s ~ sigma t].

Upper bounds ("caps"; they are used as upper bounds only, on partial and on complete configurations).
Write a_3(v) = [v ~ z] + [v ~ z'] and h_0 for the number of mate edges of kind Z.

(C1) s in X_2' :  (number of neighbours of s in mate edges of kind A) + (number of neighbours of s in X_2') <= 4 - 2 a_3(s).
     Proof: the left side is a_2(s). By (G0), a_2(s) = a_0(s) - 2 a_3(s). The set T = N(s) n X_0 avoids p (s is a
     word), T n sigma T lies in N(s) n N(sigma s), which has 2 elements, and T u sigma T lies in X_0 minus p (6 vertices):
     2 |T| - 2 <= 6, so a_0(s) <= 4.
(C2) z has at most 1 - (number of its neighbours in mate edges of kind A) neighbours in X_2', and so has z'. ((P4).)
(C3) w in N(p). Let Q = X_0 minus N[p]: 6 - 2 h_0 words in 3 - h_0 orbits, and s(w) = |N(w) n Q|. w is adjacent to at
     most one end of each orbit of Q (if w ~ q and w ~ sigma q then sigma w ~ sigma q and q: q would be adjacent to both
     ends of a mate edge), so s(w) <= 3 - h_0. a_3(w) <= 1 (w adjacent to z and z' would make z adjacent to w and
     sigma w). The number of neighbours of w in X_2' is, by (G0) at w:
        kind A:  a_0(w) = 1 + s(w) (p and Q), a_2(w) = a_0(w) - 2 a_3(w), one of them is the mate:
                 exactly s(w) - 2 a_3(w)            <=  3 - h_0 - 2 a_3(w);
        kind W:  a_0(w) = 1 + s(w), a_2(w) = a_0(w) + 3 - 2 a_3(w), all of them in X_2':
                 exactly 4 + s(w) - 2 a_3(w)        <=  7 - h_0 - 2 a_3(w);
        kind Z:  a_0(w) = 2 + s(w) (p, the mate, Q), a_2(w) = a_0(w) + 6 - 2 a_3(w), all in X_2', and
                 a_1(w) = 20 - 2 a_2(w) - 3 a_3(w) >= 0:
                 exactly 8 + s(w) - 2 a_3(w)        <=  min(11 - h_0 - 2 a_3(w), floor((20 - 3 a_3(w)) / 2)).
     If a cap is negative for the base itself, the base is rejected (this never happens in the first program).

Complete configuration on a base: 9 (resp. 8) orbits with their data such that every unit {i, j} is realised exactly
need'(i, j) times, the caps hold, and the induced graph on the 37 + 18 (resp. 37 + 16) vertices passes the budget and
semidefiniteness tests of section 5.


4. Why a search over units is exhaustive
----------------------------------------

Suppose G exists with n_3 = 2. Its base is one of the bases of section 2, and it is not rejected by any necessary test:
after a relabelling of section 6 it is one of the kept bases of stage 1. Take any state made of that base and k orbits
of X_2' of G with their true data, not all of them. Some unit has fewer realisations among the placed vertices than
need'; pick one, {i, j}, by any rule. In G an unplaced vertex s of X_2' has U_s = {i, j}. Its true data is one of the
finitely many candidates (V_s, the two neighbours in N(p), [s ~ z], [s ~ z'], the two bits to each placed orbit). The
state with the orbit of s added is again made of true orbits, so it passes every necessary test and every cap. By
induction the search reaches a complete configuration. So:

    if for every kept base the exhaustive search finds no complete configuration, the branch n_3 = 2 of case I is
    impossible.

The rule choosing the unit is free. A candidate, or a base, may be rejected only by an exact computation (section 5)
or by a cap of section 3.


5. Tests (all are necessary conditions)
---------------------------------------

- Budget. In any vertex set, for any graph all of whose edges are edges of G (the induced graph, or the induced graph
  with some adjacencies not yet decided and left out), two adjacent vertices have at most 1 common neighbour and two
  non-adjacent vertices at most 2.
- Semidefiniteness. M1 = 9 (3I - A) + J and M2 = 11 (4I + A) - 2J are positive semidefinite on all 99 vertices (they are
  63 and 77 times the projectors on the eigenspaces -4 and 3). So their principal submatrices on a vertex set whose
  induced graph is completely known are positive semidefinite. A rejection must be exact: an integer vector x with
  x^T M x < 0 computed in integers, or exact rational elimination. A floating-point test may only keep.
- Optional (the first program has it, and it did not fire in this branch so far): the rank bound. On a sigma-invariant
  vertex set, the matrices D^T M1 D (D = the vectors e_v - e_{sigma v}) and E^T M1 E (E = e_p and the vectors
  e_v + e_{sigma v}) have rank at most 22 (sigma has eigenvalues +1 and -1 with multiplicity 22 each on the eigenspace -4).


6. Symmetry (optional)
----------------------

The following relabellings preserve the model, so one base per class is enough:
- an automorphism g of B applied to the indices of S+ and S- together (it maps the type (U3 ; V3) to (g U3 ; g V3) and
  every type of mate edge to its image);
- the exchange of the names z and z' (the type (U3 ; V3) becomes (V3 ; U3); the attachment {w1, w2} becomes
  {sigma w1, sigma w2});
- the order of the seven mate edges, and which vertex of a mate edge is called w (naming only). In particular the two
  vertices of a mate edge of kind Z are interchangeable: attaching z to one or to the other gives the same base.
The first program takes the types (U3 ; V3) up to Aut(B) x (z <-> z') (section 8), then, for a fixed type, the
N-configurations up to the group G_z of the automorphisms of B that preserve the pair {U3, V3} (those exchanging U3 and
V3 are composed with the exchange z <-> z', which does not change an N-configuration), and for each representative
N-configuration all the attachments (only one vertex of a mate edge of kind Z is offered). The classes of bases are
compared between programs as "canonical keys": the smallest image, under G_z with the exchange, of the list of the
seven typed mate edges, each with a mark 0 / 1 / 2 (no vertex adjacent to z / the vertex adjacent to x_U resp. x_i is
adjacent to z / the other one is), the mark of a mate edge of kind Z being 0 or 1.
